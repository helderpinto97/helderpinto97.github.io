I am a postdoctoral researcher in Daniele Marinazzo's group at Ghent University, working on information-theoretic methods for analysing how physiological and neural systems interact. My current project asks whether higher-order structure in EEG — the balance between synergy and redundancy — tracks recovery after stroke.

My research develops measures that quantify how information is transferred, stored and modified between signals such as heart period, blood pressure, respiration and intracranial pressure — and then decomposes those measures so that a change in coupling can be traced to a specific mechanism rather than reported as coupling in general. A recurring theme is making these estimators work on real recordings: short, non-stationary, and carrying the long-range correlations that standard models ignore. More on the research page.

I completed my PhD in Applied Mathematics in 2026 at the Faculty of Sciences of the University of Porto, supervised by Ana Paula Rocha and Luca Faes, on an FCT doctoral studentship. Before that I read Mathematics and then Mathematical Engineering at the same faculty. Much of my work is collaborative: with Luca Faes' group in Palermo on information-theoretic methods, with Celeste Dias at the Neurocritical Care Unit of São João Hospital on multimodal neuromonitoring, and with Michal Javorka in Martin on cardiovascular physiology.
