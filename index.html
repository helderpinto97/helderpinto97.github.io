I am a postdoctoral researcher in Prof. Daniele Marinazzo's group at Ghent University. I work on information-theoretic methods for analysing how physiological and neural systems interact. My current project asks whether the balance between synergy and redundancy in EEG tracks recovery after stroke.

I come from applied mathematics: probability, statistics and stochastic processes, applied to problems in biomedical engineering. I combine multivariate time series analysis with information theory in a network physiology framework, working on brain-heart interactions and cardiovascular crosstalk. My recent work quantifies higher-order interactions in complex networks, where the unit of analysis is a group of signals rather than a pair. The goal is to assess risk in autonomic stress and critical illness. More on the research page.

I graduated in Mathematics from the Faculty of Sciences of the University of Porto in 2019 and completed an MSc in Mathematical Engineering there in 2021, on a grant from the Centre of Mathematics. My PhD in Applied Mathematics followed at the same faculty, funded by FCT and supervised by Prof. Ana Paula Rocha and Prof. Luca Faes from the University of Palermo. Throughout, I taught as an invited assistant in medicine, artificial intelligence and mathematics programmes.

I work with hospitals and with international groups in biomedical signal processing: with Dr. Celeste Dias, at the Faculty of Medicine in Porto and the Neurocritical Care Unit of São João Hospital, on multimodal neuromonitoring; and with Dr. Michal Javorka at Comenius University in Martin on cardiovascular physiology.
